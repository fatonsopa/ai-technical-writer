# Output grading

Define case-specific expected properties before generation. Score each applicable dimension 0–3: 0 materially wrong/unusable; 1 needs substantial correction; 2 usable with minor correction; 3 meets the request without material correction. Require at least 2 in every applicable dimension and all mandatory case checks. Mark inapplicable dimensions with a reason. Writing Style (WS) applies to all authored text, including short labels, questions and intermediate messages; assess language for every output. Apply its preservation rules to exact literals rather than declaring style inapplicable.

Dimensions: factual grounding/uncertainty; preservation of meaning/obligations/literals; type-specific completeness; reader usefulness and appropriate detail; natural connected language; scope/routing; requirements/design/work/verification consistency where relevant.

Invented evidence, approval, execution, verified paths, or material requirement changes are critical failures. Style cannot compensate. Empty headings do not satisfy structure. Equivalent prose is acceptable; exact strings are required only for actual contracts and literals.

Calibrate graders using labeled good/flawed samples, including concise correct writing, verbose incompleteness, fabricated passes, and omitted exceptions. Author-assigned labels are not independent human calibration. Hide variant identity and vary answer order for comparisons. Keep acceptance cases out of tuning. Record all attempts, settings, outputs, available traces, scores/reasons, and infrastructure failures. Self-review is not independent generation; a passing sample does not prove future reliability.

For diagrams, grade the type-specific notation, visible title/subtitle and symbol legend in the actual export, plus the overview and element explanations. Apply the writing guide to captions and labels. Hidden metadata or a syntax pass cannot establish screenshot context, readable layout or correct meaning. A text-only review can identify reported problems but must not claim visual inspection.

Use the captured Writing Style (WS) guide for documents, titles, tables, diagram labels/metadata/legends, explanations, questions/options, audit findings, recommendations, examples and delivery text. Assess actual public messages in the supplied trace as well as the final output. Missing traces limit observed coverage; a final-document pass cannot establish the style of unseen messages. Do not accept “only a diagram,” “only a title,” or “not prose” as a style exemption. A guide-read declaration or review receipt alone does not establish good writing.

Explicit mandatory conventions are acceptance conditions. For example, Writing Style (WS) requires the full document name followed by its acronym whenever authored text refers to that document type, including public messages. A confirmed violation blocks acceptance; a general language score cannot waive it. Preserve the guide’s exceptions for literal filenames, code identifiers, input aliases, quotations and external publication titles.

Primary document and diagram titles name a specific subject; action titles belong to tasks or steps. Judge whether a title identifies the actual content, not whether it merely fits a word limit. A diagram subtitle briefly states scope. Reject inflated, vague or misleading wording. For an unfamiliar reader, require an overview at one level of detail. An explicitly technical view may include necessary detail, but must explain its reader need; changing its profile does not cure a confusing diagram. Preserve relevant exceptions in prose or a separately scoped view.
