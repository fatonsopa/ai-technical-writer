# Product Brief (PB)

A product brief helps an owner decide whether a problem is worth defining as a product change. Write one while the direction is still being shaped, before a [Product Requirements Document (PRD)](product_requirements_document.md). A full PRD at this stage makes unknowns look settled; a brief keeps them visible.

## Principles

- Start from evidence: an observed problem, a request or a [Discovery Interview Record (DIR)](discovery_interview.md). If the intent isn't clear from the sources, run discovery first.
- Name who is affected and the outcome they need. Describe the problem, not a solution.
- State the decision the reader has to make, usually whether to proceed to product definition.
- Mark what isn't known. A missing baseline, target or budget stays an open item; never fill it in. Include an appetite, the time or effort the owner is willing to spend, only when the owner supplied it.
- List non-goals and unresolved choices so the next author doesn't absorb them.

## Structure

Keep it short enough to read in a few minutes. A reader who wasn't in the conversation should be able to decide whether to proceed from the brief alone. Deliver it through the checked-delivery workflow; the PRD author can then cite it.

## Template

```md
# [Problem or opportunity]

## Decision
[What the reader decides, and what happens if they proceed or don't.]

## Problem and who is affected
[Observed problem, the people affected and the evidence for it.]

## Desired outcome
[What should be true for those people; no invented target.]

## Non-goals
- [Excluded work.]

## Unknowns and open choices
- [Missing baseline, target or decision, and who can supply it.]

## Sources
- [Request, interview record, observation or data, with dates.] [Appetite, if the owner supplied one.]
```

## Quality check

Can a reader name who is affected, the decision and what is still unknown, without the original conversation? Check that every figure has a source and that no budget, target or approval has been invented.
